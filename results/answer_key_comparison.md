# Comparison with the answer key (instructor log files)

The final outputs for every set, including the unknown sets, were committed before the log files were opened: PA3 in commit 3db24bf, PA4 in abeadb4 and PA5 in 28d85aa. This comparison reads those committed files and does not change them. Nothing in the code or the options was adjusted after looking at the logs.

The full tables, for the debug sets as well as the unknown sets, are in [`answer_key_tables.md`](answer_key_tables.md) and are generated by

```
python -m cisreg.answer_key
```

Our F_reg is recovered from each output file by registering our pointer tips d_k onto the written s_k. The log gives the instructor's computed F_reg and the actual F_reg used to generate the data. Frame errors are the rotation angle and translation of F1^-1 F2.

## Two facts about the logs

- **The log's "RMS residual" is the mean distance.** For PA3, where F_reg = I for both programs and the points agree to 0.01 mm, our mean of |d_k - c_k| matches the log value for every set (for example G-Unknown 1.6630 against 1.6614, J-Unknown 2.3178 against 2.3180), while our RMS is about 15 to 20% higher. The residual columns below therefore compare our mean with the log value.
- **The compact `Fr([r],[p]]` form uses the rotation vector in radians.** This was checked against the matrix blocks (`tests/test_answer_key.py`); all entries have matrix blocks, which are used.

## PA3 unknown sets

The actual F_reg is the identity for G, H and J, as assumed in PA3. Our mean residuals match the log: G 1.6630 against 1.6614 mm, H 1.5377 against 1.5358 mm, J 2.3178 against 2.3180 mm.

## Discussion

**Every unknown set is registered correctly.** On the noise-free sets our F_reg is within 0.006 degrees and 0.003 mm of the true one, and on the noisy sets within 0.075 degrees and 0.018 mm. Our noise-free PA5 weights are within 0.1 of the truth, and the noisy ones within 0.6.

**Noise-free sets: limited by the rounding of the input files.** The instructor's noise-free results are essentially exact (residual 0.0000, frame errors about 0.0001 degrees, weight errors about 0.001), ours are not (residual about 0.003 mm, frame errors up to 0.006 degrees, weight errors up to 0.09). This matches what the debug sets showed: the readings we are given are rounded to 0.01 mm, and the instructor's program evidently used the unrounded values. Two checks support this:

- With the rounded readings, our solution fits the data better than the true parameters do. The sum of squared distances to the deformed surface is 0.00297 mm^2 with our F_reg and weights against 0.00360 with the actual ones for PA5 G-Unknown, and 0.00238 against 0.00297 for K-Unknown (0.00103 against 0.00188 for A-Debug). So the remaining error is in the data, not in the optimization.
- Perturbing the readings at their rounding level (uniform, plus or minus 0.005 mm, 10 trials) moves the PA5 G-Unknown weights by a standard deviation of 0.018 to 0.052 (largest change 0.114), and K-Unknown by 0.021 to 0.026 (largest 0.054). Our weight errors of 0.092 for G and 0.065 for K are about two to three of these standard deviations, which is plausible for the largest of six weights. Re-solving G with a 100 times tighter stopping tolerance changes its weights by only 0.0005, so the solver had converged.

**Noisy sets: comparable to the instructor's program.** With 0.1 mm marker noise neither program recovers the truth exactly, and their errors are of the same size. For PA4 our rotation error is smaller than the instructor's on E-Debug (0.019 against 0.030 degrees) and F-Debug (0.053 against 0.055), and larger on J-Unknown (0.058 against 0.042) and K-Unknown (0.074 against 0.066). For the PA5 weights ours are worse on H-Unknown (0.60 against 0.50) and E-Debug and better on J-Unknown (0.47 against 0.65) and F-Debug. Our mean residuals are equal to or slightly below the instructor's (for example 0.0631 against 0.0665 mm for PA4 K-Unknown), consistent with the finding in `pa4_validation.md` that our fits reach a slightly lower sum of squares.

**Iteration counts.** The instructor's PA4 runs took 2 to 101 rigid iterations, ours 17 to 109 (the higher minimum is the slow creep caused by the rounded inputs). The instructor's PA5 runs report exactly 50 rigid, 2 mode and 1 combined step for every set, which looks like a fixed schedule; ours used 91 to 167 rigid ICP iterations, 30 mode steps and 29 to 80 combined steps until the 1e-5 mm stopping test was met.

## Summary

All unknown-set results agree with the answer key to within the limits set by the data. On noise-free sets they are within the precision allowed by the 0.01 mm rounding of the input files, and on noisy sets the errors are comparable to those of the instructor's own program. There is no unknown set where our registration or shape estimate is wrong.
